# Case study brief: The East India Company, Britain’s fiscal empire and revolutionary protest

**Working version:** October 19, 2026 · **Research scope:** India, Great Britain and British North America, primarily 1757–1775

## Guiding question

How did British global warfare, government debt, corporate financial distress, parliamentary policy and colonial constitutional disputes interact? What evidence connects them across different regions?

## Findings already supported by online institutional sources

1. **Global war, different regional consequences.** Britain’s Seven Years’ War victories included fighting in India and North America. The 1763 Treaty of Paris redistributed territory and imposed new frontier governance problems. [US State Department: Paris](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1750-1775/treaty-of-paris).
2. **Postwar fiscal pressure.** British war debt informed revenue-raising policies; this was one factor in the Stamp Act and the wider controversy about Parliament’s power to tax the colonies. [State Department: Parliamentary taxation](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1750-1775/parliamentary-taxation).
3. **Indigenous sovereignty was not a footnote.** Pontiac’s resistance, western land companies and Britain’s Proclamation of 1763 reflected conflicting attempts to govern or claim territory; neither colonists nor Indigenous peoples formed a homogeneous interest group. [State Department: Frontier policy](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1750-1775/proclamation-line-1763).
4. **Company relief became an American political dispute.** Parliament enacted the 1773 Tea Act to assist the financially troubled East India Company while keeping the existing tea duty; merchant interests and objections to taxation without representation formed distinct grievances. [Library of Congress: Colonial rebellion](https://www.loc.gov/classroom-materials/united-states-history-primary-source-timeline/american-revolution-1763-1783/colonies-rebellion-1773-1774/).
5. **A documented sequence of responses.** The Boston Tea Party on 16 December 1773 was followed by the Coercive Acts in 1774, Congress's political coordination and fighting in April 1775. The sequence is historical; attributing the entire American Revolution to the Company is not warranted. [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/collections/continental-congress-and-constitutional-convention-from-1774-to-1789/articles-and-essays/timeline/1773-to-1774/) · [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/mima/learn/historyculture/april-19-1775.htm).
6. **A direct rhetorical connection across continents.** A New York broadside in 1773 drew explicit comparisons between the Company's alleged misconduct in India and feared effects of its American tea privileges. Its famine figures and accusations remain statements by partisan contemporaries, **not** separately verified findings of this project. [Library of Congress, Printed Ephemera](https://www.loc.gov/resource/rbpe.10501300/?sp=2&st=text).

## Interpretive boundaries

- Direct policy responses, concurrent fiscal pressures and rhetorical analogies must have different relationship types.
- The Tea Act is not evidence that East India Company directors intentionally designed the American Revolution.
- The fact that several British imperial decisions occurred in 1773 does **not** prove a centralized multi-generation plan.
- A retrospective account from a US government institution is valuable but not a substitute for British legislative records, contemporaneous Indian sources or Indigenous oral and written histories.
- Colonial protest leaders could sincerely oppose taxation while participating in settler dispossession and slavery; future analysis needs independent evidence before drawing individual-level conclusions.
- European victory and colonial independence narratives may marginalize Indigenous nations, enslaved populations, women, and communities affected by Company rule in Bengal.

## Primary records to inspect next

- East India Company financial petitions and stock/share registers, 1772–1773.
- Tea Act, Regulating Act and Parliamentary debates with exact provisions and vote records.
- Contemporary Boston and Philadelphia letters on perceptions of the East India Company.
- Bengal revenue administration and famine history through South Asian scholarly and archival sources.
- Records of Indigenous diplomacy, treaties and resistance before and after the 1763 Proclamation.
- Merchant financing, insurance and tea consignees: investigate **individual transactions** rather than presume a continuous coordinated network.
