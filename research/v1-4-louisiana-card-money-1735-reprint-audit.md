# V1.4: 14 September 1735 Louisiana card-money ordinance — a legal reprint, not a debt receipt

**Reviewed 3 November 2026.** This is a bounded source-level addition to [issue #24](https://github.com/mcfruitpunch/Anatomy-of-civilization/issues/24), not a solution to the still-unread **24 February 1731 Company debt decree**. The 1735 ordinance is a distinct downstream royal monetary policy, **not** a Company creditor settlement.

## Exact accessible witness and provenance

Ernest Zay, *Histoire monétaire des colonies françaises d'après les documents officiels* (1892), printed **pp. 181–183**, reproduces an ordinance headed **Versailles, 14 September 1735** on Louisiana card money. Accessible digitized 1892 edition: [Wikimedia/University of Toronto scan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/dd/Histoire_mon%C3%A9taire_des_colonies_fran%C3%A7aises%2C_d%27apr%C3%A8s_les_documents_officiels_-_Ernest_Zay_-_%C3%89dition_1892.pdf). **What was examined:** the digitized 1892 text extraction for pp.181–183, including articles I–VI. **What was NOT examined:** the original 1735 royal imprint, signed archival decree, registration entry, fabrication records, issued cards, receipts or exchange-bill ledgers. This is a later edition of primary legal wording, not an original eighteenth-century witness.

A [BnF-derived scan mirror](https://frenchbooksonindia.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/1892_Histoire-mon%C3%A9taire-des-colonies-fran%C3%A7aises-dapr%C3%A8s-les-documents-officiels...-...-_12148.pdf) has an **OCR discrepancy**: its text extraction renders the heading as **“260 m.”**, while the Wikimedia scan's text extraction reads **“200 m.”**, and articles **I, IV and V** consistently give **200 m.** (200,000) in both transcriptions. These are *two digital transcriptions of the same 1892 publication*, not independent original documents. The likely OCR error is not an established variant of the 1735 law; inspect the printed page image and an eighteenth-century original before making that claim.

## Clause-level reading of the 1892 reproduction

| Location | Reproduced legal content | Evidence classification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| p.181, preamble | Crown says gold and silver sent after the 1731 retrocession had returned to France, impairing commerce; claims the colony's inhabitants wanted card money. | **Contemporary royal assertions transmitted in 1892**; not audited specie flows, independent inhabitants' testimony or consent. |
| p.182, art. I | Permits **200,000 livres nominal value** of card money in specified denominations, to bear royal arms and marine controller's signature. | **Authorized ceiling**, not evidence 200,000 were actually made, circulated or spent. |
| p.182, arts. II–III | Additional signatures/countersignatures required by denomination. | Official validation design, not verified signed cards. |
| p.182, art. IV | Four procès-verbaux required for each fabrication: for governor, commissaire ordonnateur, New Orleans control office and Marine secretary. | **Reproducible original-accounting lead**: find these registers before asserting fabrication. |
| p.182, art. V | Forbids exceeding 200,000 livres. | Statutory limit, not actual issuance. |
| pp.182–183, art. VI | Sets intended circulation and acceptance at royal warehouses and for bills of exchange drawn on Marine treasury. | Legal acceptance rules, **not** a proven exchange, warehouse sale, redemption or payment. |
| p.183, closing | Names Bienville and Salmon as officials directed to enforce and register the ordinance. | Administrative assignment, **not** a personal payment or proof of implementation. |

The short clause “Il sera fabriqué pour la somme de 200 m. liv.” (p.182, art. I) is prospective legal language. It must never be drawn as an arrow transferring 200,000 livres from Crown to Company, from Crown to residents, or to a named payee.

## Interpretive limits and affected communities

**Documented in the examined 1892 edition:** it reproduces legal instructions dated 14 September 1735, with a 200,000-livre ceiling and specified signatures, records and acceptance mechanisms.

**Interpretation:** a later royal currency policy could be a response to liquidity pressures after the Company's withdrawal, but the causal chain and actual currency issuance require independent records. This is **not** evidence that the Crown assumed corporate liabilities under the February 1731 decree.

**Unresolved:** original 1735 law's wording and any typographic differences; registration and production registers; actual currency manufactured, distributed, accepted or redeemed; who bore losses; whether any Company creditor's claim was paid. The original February 1731 decree remains unread due to Gallica access restrictions.

The royal text's claim that “inhabitants” desired the policy is **not** evidence of consultation with Choctaw, Natchez or other sovereign nations, or with enslaved people. Read against [Choctaw Nation's community-authored history](https://www.choctawnation.com/news/iti-fabvssa/fort-tombecbe-and-early-choctaw-treaties/) and the [Louisiana Historical Center's warning](https://www.lacolonialdocs.org/post/8/the-process) that colonial indexing omits many women's and enslaved people's names. Neither royal administrative language nor a currency ceiling establishes legitimate consent or a complete account of social consequences.
