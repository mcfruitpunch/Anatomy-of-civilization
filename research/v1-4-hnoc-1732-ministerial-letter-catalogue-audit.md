# V1.4 — HNOC item 7620: a 1732 ministerial letter's catalogue abstract (9 November 2026)

Related: #24. This is a **catalogue-level source audit and access lead**, not a transcription of the original letter and not evidence of a completed payment.

## Institutional record directly examined

Historic New Orleans Collection, *A Guide to French Louisiana Manuscripts*, Surrey Calendar item **7620**:

https://surrey.hnoc.org/full_notice.php?id_document=7620

The notice identifies a two-page letter dated **2 September 1732**, at Marly, from the Minister of Marine to Salmon. Its archival citation is **AC B 57:815**, updated as **ANOM B 57:815**. The HNOC notice also gives a cross-citation to the Library of Congress, Center for Louisiana Studies, and lists microfilm there. It does not provide a reel number or a separate digital image link.

**Directly observed in the HNOC abstract:** among several subjects, the abstract lists (1) Macmahon's sale of people enslaved by the Company of the Indies and (2) approval of a Superior Council decision in the case of Diron and the Company. It also lists councillor appointments, Bruslé as Company agent, a shipment of plants for the royal garden, and Bernac's commission. The HNOC's historical racial term is not adopted as a description of people.

## What is documented, and what is not

- **Documented at catalogue level:** HNOC's abstract attributes both the Company-enslaved people sale subject and the Diron–Company decision to the same two-page ministerial letter, dated 2 September 1732; it identifies AC/ANOM B 57:815 and a Library of Congress microfilm lead.
- **Not firsthand examined:** neither page of the underlying letter was read. The HNOC abstract is a finding-aid description, not a diplomatic transcription.
- **Unresolved:** whether the abstract's wording precisely reflects the letter; when any sale occurred; who the people were; their number; purchaser; price; consideration; receipt; payment; legal authority; and whether any proceeds related to the 1731 Company debt decree.
- **No inferred connection:** the abstract co-lists the sale and the Diron decision, but does not say they were part of the same transaction or legal proceeding. The 2 September date is the letter date, not a proven date of sale.
- **No ledger change:** this source does not establish a payer, payee, liability transfer, settlement, or amount. The separate 29,000-livre Diron award remains a legal-award claim, not a verified disbursement.

## Affected-community perspective and limits

The letter is a French ministry record and the catalogue preserves an administrative view. It does not preserve the names, voices, agency, or consent of the people described as enslaved. The atlas therefore refers to **people enslaved by the Company**, not merely Company inventory, and does not infer their identities or community affiliations.

For the separate Choctaw diplomatic context, the Choctaw Nation of Oklahoma's Iti Fabvssa article, “Fort Tombecbe and Early Choctaw Treaties” (3 April 2023), describes Choctaw leaders as active negotiators who sought trade relationships and posts within their homeland; it records that women played vital roles in Choctaw diplomacy and distinguishes the 1736 Fort Tombecbe from earlier trading-post activity. This community-authored account is relevant to Choctaw agency, but it is **not** evidence about the people in the 1732 sale reference or the contents of B 57:815.
