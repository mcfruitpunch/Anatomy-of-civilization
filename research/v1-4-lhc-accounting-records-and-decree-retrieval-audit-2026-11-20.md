# Louisiana State Museum archival cross-check: Company accounting and the 1731 decree
**Research date:** 2026-11-20  
**Repository target:** V1.4, Issue #24  
**Review level:** Institutional finding aids and catalogue metadata examined; no new underlying manuscript or printed decree pages examined.

## Why this milestone
The February 24, 1731 Company-debt decree remains unread. Rather than repeat the failed Gallica retrieval, this pass checked the Louisiana State Museum Historical Center's detailed finding aids for earlier Company accounting rules, audit authority, creditor enforcement and records representing enslaved people's legal agency.

## Directly examined institutional finding aids

### RG3 — French Manuscripts, Mississippi Valley
The June 2013 Louisiana State Museum finding aid identifies RG3 as one box and 40 folders, accession 1852.1–1852.40, dated 1679–1769. It says the collection is on loan from the Louisiana Historical Society and open for research. The contents list provides two especially relevant records:

- **Accession 1852.30, 11 December 1725, four pages:** a resolution concerning enslaved people furnished to residents by the Company of the Indies who had not paid; the finding-aid abstract says those residents were prohibited from selling them.
- **Accession 1852.36, 14 October 1726, two pages:** an appointment of La Chaise and Perrault to audit the accounts of the Company. The finding aid does not name the company in this item description or establish that the audit was completed.

The 2024 LSM manuscript-collections guide independently repeats the RG3 summary, including the audit appointment.

### RG377 — Louisiana French Colonial Period Documents
The August 2022 RG377 finding aid identifies accession T025.2003.112 as photocopied Louisiana French colonial documents from Library of Congress holdings, dated 1704–1731, extent four linear feet/nine boxes, and open for research.

It lists **record 1729021501**, dated 15 February 1729, *Instructions for general and special bookkeepers in Louisiana*, Series B43:846–864, 43 sheets. The finding-aid summary says the instructions address active and inactive debts, payment of debts of the “old company,” payments in silver and bills, ship expenses and slave cargoes. The identity of the “old company” is not established by the summary.

### A negative inventory check — not proof of absence
I searched the RG377 published contents list for “February 24, 1731,” “February 1731” and “17310224.” No matching entry was found. The last listed item is **1731022801**, dated 28 February 1731, a 12-sheet sailing instruction for Sieur Kerguenalle, Series B43:916–921, listed under Box 8.

A separate discrepancy remains: the finding-aid summary says **nine boxes**, but the online contents list has no **Box 9** heading. This may reflect an unlisted ninth box, an omitted contents section or an outdated extent statement. It is not proof that the February 24 decree is absent from the physical collection. The next request should ask the archivist to reconcile the box count and check unlisted or supplemental material.

### RG1 — Judicial Records of the French Superior Council
The LSM's 2024 manuscript-collections guide describes RG1 (accessions 1848 and 1867; 1714–1769; 108 linear feet, 216 boxes and eight oversize boxes). It says most cases concern creditors seeking recovery of unpaid debts and that the collection includes successions, commercial records and petitions by enslaved people requesting manumission. No individual case was identified or examined in this pass; the guide does not specify access conditions.

This is a high-value route for researching both creditor enforcement and the experiences and legal agency of enslaved people. It should not be reduced to a debtor-name index.

## Alternative route to the 1733 printed decree
The Columbia Law Library catalogue identifies the two-page 1733 printing as LLMC record **LLMC1338688796** (Columbia record 1505745). University of Washington Libraries lists LLMC Digital in its History Digital Collections guide; its link redirects through UW's Shibboleth authentication. This is an institutional route for an authenticated UW user, not public access, and availability of this exact record in the UW instance has not been confirmed.

## What is established, and what is not
**Documented at finding-aid level:** the existence and catalogue descriptions of the 1725 resolution, 1726 audit appointment and 1729 bookkeeping instructions; the RG1 collection-level description; the RG377 published-inventory search result and its nine-box/Box-9 discrepancy.

**Not established:** the original French wording, completed audit, named debtors, actual account balances, collection of debts, payments, settlements, Crown assumption of Company liabilities, or any direct legal connection between these earlier records and the 24 February 1731 decree.

**Human context:** the 1725 abstract describes enslaved people through the colonial administration's property language. It is not their testimony. The RG1 guide's reference to manumission petitions is a reason to seek and read those petitions alongside Company orders, not to treat people as neutral accounting entries. Existing Choctaw- and Peoria-authored context remains separate; French administrative descriptions do not establish Indigenous consent.

## Exact next archival requests
1. Request the four pages of RG3 accession 1852.30 and the two pages of RG3 accession 1852.36; confirm the issuing authority, company identity and whether any audit report survives.
2. Request all 43 sheets of RG377 record 1729021501 and the underlying Library of Congress series/call number.
3. Ask the LHC archivist to reconcile RG377's stated nine-box extent with the published list's missing Box 9 heading and check any unlisted or misdated 24 February 1731 material.
4. Ask for an RG1 index search for Company of Indies notes/bills, creditor suits and successions dated 1731–1736, and separately for manumission petitions.
5. For the standalone 1733 debt decree, use LLMC1338688796 only through a confirmed subscriber route or request a reproduction from Columbia/LLMC.

No new financial-flow entry was created. The 1731 decree remains catalogue-only; no debt amount, payment, settlement or liability transfer was added.
