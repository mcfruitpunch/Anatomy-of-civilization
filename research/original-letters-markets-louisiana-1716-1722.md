# V0.9 — Paris, London, Amsterdam and French Louisiana: an original-record inquiry

**Investigation date:** 19 October 2026  
**Period:** 1716–1722 (main documentary inventory 1716–1721)  
**Status:** archival catalogue and institutional-image survey; no claim of completed name-by-name transcription.

## Executive finding

There are independently locatable original laws, public-market prints, contemporary British criticism, Dutch price/transaction ledgers, passenger lists and colonial censuses for these years. They support specific historical **channels** through which financial ideas and commerce were discussed and recorded. None of the currently inspected records establishes a **named private instruction or direct personal letter among John Law, John Blunt and an Amsterdam investor**. This is an important negative research result: the question remains open, not silently converted into a verified connection.

## 1. France: original manuscript rules and administrative acts

The [Historic New Orleans Collection's John Law manuscript and edict collection](https://hnoc.org/research-collections/collection-highlights/john-law-collection) documents these *specific* originals, with object numbers:
- **2 May 1716 — HNOC 2010.0158.1**: manuscript letters patent chartering the General Bank, authenticated in museum description as signed under Louis XV and Regent Philippe d'Orléans. This is a **state charter**, not a private shareholder letter.
- **20 April 1720 — HNOC 2023.0082.2**: Council of State order restricting bank exchanges of paper notes for large silver coins. It establishes promulgation of the restriction, **not the actual number of coins exchanged**.
- **30 August 1720 — HNOC 2021.0237.21**: royal order appointing officials Baussan and Bertin to account for specified banknotes and ensure their destruction. The **amount burned was not proved** by reading the museum summary.
- HNOC's collection also holds correspondence, over two hundred historical papers, and nineteen 1720 administrative orders; individual transcriptions have **not** yet been checked. [Collection page](https://hnoc.org/research-collections/collection-highlights/john-law-collection) links a finding aid (MSS 606).

This documentary progression is stronger than a generalized 'money was manipulated' claim: it identifies legally enacted restrictions, dates, objects, and the absence of transaction proof.

## 2. London and Amsterdam: publications and original trade data

- **London, 1720:** Wellcome's catalogue contains a [pamphlet attributed to Archibald Hutcheson](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/yy3prd74) responding to financial calculations printed in the *White-Hall Evening-Post*. The *existence, author attribution and subject* are confirmed in the catalogue. We did not read the full pamphlet and therefore do **not** attribute uninspected numerical formulas or personal motives to its author.
- **English language, 1720:** [BnF's John Law primary collection](https://heritage.bnf.fr/france-ameriques/en/john-law) lists *A full and impartial account of the company of Mississipi, otherwise call'd the french East-India company, projected and settled by Mr. Law*. This is **direct evidence that an English-language text about the French scheme existed**; the Gallica scan link was inaccessible in this research session, and publication place or individual readership has not been confirmed. Do not use this record to assert that the South Sea directors personally read it.
- **Amsterdam, circa 1720:** [Wellcome's original printed satire, reference 811589i](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/gvz5tu3g), depicts an English coffee house in Amsterdam with John Law imagery and a Paris-Quincampoix reference. A [second print, 812076i](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/e32vdtk5), depicts traders and rising/falling share fortunes. These are **cultural artifacts**—not a list of historical counterparties or evidence of a named secret meeting.
- **Leiden, 20 November 1719–30 December 1720:** [Yale's South Sea Bubble 1720 Project](https://som.yale.edu/centers/international-center-for-finance/data/historical-financial-research-data/south-seas-bubble-1720) holds scanned *Leydse Courant* issues, with later 1721 issues also available. The individual newspaper issues have **not** been transcribed to identify when a particular London or Paris financial announcement appeared.
- **Amsterdam/Rotterdam:** Yale's project indexes securities quoted in Dutch markets, British shares quoted there, foreign-exchange prices, and the Bank of Amsterdam agio. Its *Stad Rotterdam* ledger and transaction books begin in **July 1720**. **We have not yet read a dated individual transaction row** or matched a counterparty across countries.

**Copyright and data use:** Yale makes downloads available for academic research but restricts commercial use and redistribution without permission. Our open-source project therefore links directly to Yale and does **not** republish its spreadsheet, Dutch newspaper scans or ledger images.

## 3. Louisiana: exactly which original passenger and population files exist?

The [Archives nationales d'outre-mer's online index](https://nouvelle-france.org/eng/item/details?id=31224) contains a **68-image passenger list**: **COL G1 464**, compiled at Lorient and dated 24 January 1721, covering entries from 4 January 1720 to 24 January 1721. **An indexed list is not evidence of arrival, survival, consent or whether any particular recorded person was enslaved**.

Additional original census files indexed at the same repository:
- **28 June 1721** — [Fort Louis de la Mobile census, record 31234](https://nouvelle-france.org/eng/item/details?id=31234), COL G1 464, seven images; associated with official Diron, prepared at Biloxi. Catalogued categories include colonial household and enslaved people, but **we have not verified any numbers or names from the seven pages**.
- **24 November 1721** — [New Orleans and nearby settlements census, record 31235](https://nouvelle-france.org/eng/item/details?id=31235), COL G1 464, twelve images. The catalogue identifies signatures of Diron and colleagues and says it accompanied a **letter dated 2 January 1722 from Diron**. This is a lead to specific administrative correspondence; **the letter's text or recipient is not independently verified**.

These materials were produced by French colonial administrators. They categorize enslaved Africans and other people through state economic and racial categories and can omit people or misdescribe their status. They **cannot substitute** for the history of Indigenous nations and enslaved communities. Historian [Cécile Vidal](https://heritage.bnf.fr/france-ameriques/en/new-orleans-1718-1769) recounts Indigenous advisers who helped Bienville choose the New Orleans site and African people held in slavery whose labor was used to build the early city. [BnF's study of Indigenous geographic knowledge](https://heritage.bnf.fr/france-ameriques/en/french-knowledge-indigenous-peoples) cautions that colonial enumerations often counted 'warriors' or settlements rather than including every person.

Primary census quantities from third-party volunteer transcriptions **were not entered**: the actual manuscript folios and their population definitions require checking.

## 4. Three different levels of a cross-border 'connection'

**A. Documented public circulation.** A 1720 English-language print about Law and Amsterdam printed depictions of Law indicate public interest in French speculative finance outside France. The physical locations of all editions and readership are not settled.

**B. Documented market infrastructure.** Yale preserves records of Dutch quotations for British securities, exchange rates, newspapers and Rotterdam transaction books. This confirms that financial information and securities could be recorded across markets, but precise information lead/lag and individual trades remain to be established from the rows.

**C. A plausible scholarly intellectual link.** Quinn and Turner (2020) say South Sea Company directors followed Law's public-debt-conversion example. This is a supported historical interpretation requiring closer inspection of original British minutes and dated correspondence before asserting a personal John Law–John Blunt instruction channel.

**No route has been marked as verified named personal correspondence.**

## 5. Technical representation

- `data/archival_documents.json`: eleven original documents or dated historical source series, each with its holding institution, exact identifier, evidentiary proposition, explicit limitation, and source IDs. Different `access_level` labels distinguish curator-verified images from museum catalogues and inaccessible text.
- `data/information_routes.json`: four source-specific Paris–London–Amsterdam routes tagged with `evidence_kind`, `confidence`, and `named_correspondent_verified:false`. This flag is enforced by automated validation until individually authenticated evidence supports changing it through a documented schema upgrade.
- `data/events.json`: eight new events, limited to supportable date precision.
- `data/relationships.json` and `data/dominoes.json`: links for the 1720 edicts, British public criticism and archival chronology, not proof of a criminal conspiracy.

## 6. Next archival work (priority order)

1. Obtain **HNOC MSS 606** relevant original letter entries (sender, recipient, physical folio and dated contents), not just photographs of government edicts. Identify any actual correspondence that names Paris, London or Amsterdam counterparties.
2. Select **three dated *Leydse Courant*** issues tied to major spring/summer 1720 South Sea price events; translate the Dutch reporting and compare publication days to corresponding original British reports. Record a lag only with exact publication dates.
3. Examine **Stad Rotterdam transaction book**, July 16–24, 1720, pp. 1–300, and one linked cash/journal entry in the *Grootboek*. Only with verified counterparty identities, securities and units should a money-flow record be added.
4. Open all **12 New Orleans census** folios and the **68-page Lorient passenger list**. Transcribe sample entries with privacy-sensitive and historical ethical treatment: distinguish people recorded as enslaved from indentured settlers, and identify which people are absent or unnamed.
5. Pair French administrative materials with primary **Indigenous diplomatic** or oral-history sources and archives foregrounding enslaved people's narratives. Do not force colonial administrative categories into identity claims.
6. Inspect complete text of the **1720 English-language Mississippi pamphlet** and Hutcheson's South Sea valuation writing. Identify printed claims and dates without assuming private correspondence.

### Research conclusion

**Public discourse between the three markets is evident; named direct correspondence and cross-country matched personal financial transfers are not established yet.** Louisiana colonial censuses and movement records exist, but their individual contents and human experiences must not be reconstructed from titles alone. That is a research result worthy of publication because it prevents unsupported claims from contaminating the long-term atlas.
