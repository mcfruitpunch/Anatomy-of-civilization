# V1.4: A 1731 captive-voyage account with contradictory reported figures (2 November 2026)

## Why this belongs in the retrocession study

Issue [#24](https://github.com/mcfruitpunch/Anatomy-of-civilization/issues/24) asks whose rights, properties and obligations changed during the 1731 Company of the Indies withdrawal **and** what happened to affected people. The French Crown/Company's legal papers alone cannot describe enslaved people's experiences or the separate sovereignty of Indigenous nations. This bounded milestone addresses a human-history evidence gap, **not** the unresolved corporate debt clauses.

## Source examination, separated by level

1. **Directly examined: a modern online historical synthesis**, [*Esclavage aux Antilles*, Martinique](https://esclavage-antilles.fr/territoires/martinique.html), section beginning “En 1731, le navire Saint-Louis de la Compagnie des Indes…”, identified as point 237 in the viewed index. It attributes its Saint-Louis episode to **Robert Harms, *The Diligent: A Voyage Through the Worlds of the Slave Trade* (2002), pp. 335–336**. The synthesis describes a Company ship carrying captive people via Martinique toward Louisiana in 1731, with people ordered ashore because of sickness and food shortages. **This is a reading of the synthesis, not Harms's actual pages or an original 1731 voyage record.**
2. **Directly examined: publisher metadata**, [Hachette/Basic Books](https://www.hachettebookgroup.com/titles/robert-harms/the-diligent/9780465028726/), confirming Harms, *The Diligent*, Basic Books, 2002, ISBN 9780465028726. The publisher's summary confirms the study's broader Atlantic slave-trade subject, **not** the Saint-Louis episode or numbers on pp.335–336.
3. **Primary-source status: not examined.** Neither a ship's log, original Company correspondence, port-health record, nor Harms's cited notes and archival locators were retrieved. No original French transcription is claimed.

## Quantitative contradiction: attributed figures, not reconstructed people

The modern synthesis reports four numbers from the Harms account: **340** captive people aboard; **92** ordered ashore initially; **10** more ordered ashore later; **229** reported to continue toward Louisiana. These are four *attributed secondary figures*, not four independently verified original counts. They do **not** reconcile: 340 − 92 − 10 = 238, whereas the stated onward figure is 229. The arithmetic difference of nine **is not evidence of nine otherwise undocumented individuals, deaths, sales, transfers or survivors**. Neither a total reaching Louisiana nor the ultimate fate of those left ashore can be derived. The online synthesis itself notes the mismatch and reports that Harms regarded their later fate as undocumented. Do not convert a historian's speculation about mortality into a documented outcome.

The structured audit in `data/louisiana_human_context_evidence.json` stores only those **attributed, contradictory claims** and explicit false verification flags; it is not a population census or financial ledger. Its validator rejects invented reconciliation, payment, Crown transfer and individual-outcome fields.

## Documented, interpretation, unresolved

- **Documented about consulted sources:** the online synthesis presents the Saint-Louis account with a Harms page reference; the publisher verifies Harms's bibliographic metadata; the BnF/Columbia catalogues identify the separate 24 February 1731 debt act, dated 1731 although one edition was printed in 1733.
- **Interpretation:** a reported captive voyage during the same year as retrocession is an important research lead for studying human consequences of Company operations. Chronological overlap **does not** prove that a particular captive person or obligation transferred from Company to Crown.
- **Unresolved:** original voyage dates, counts, individuals, destinations, whether the vessel or its cargo was included in any royal transfer, and any link to the 24 February 1731 debt decree. None of these are encoded as historical facts.

**Distinct Indigenous perspectives:** [Choctaw Nation's Iti Fabvssa (3 April 2023)](https://www.choctawnation.com/news/iti-fabvssa/fort-tombecbe-and-early-choctaw-treaties/) describes Choctaw diplomatic agency and 1729 Mobile negotiations. That history is **not** evidence about the Saint-Louis voyage; the two must be represented alongside each other without implying Choctaw responsibility for colonial enslavement or French debts. Do not presume French retrocession established Indigenous consent.
