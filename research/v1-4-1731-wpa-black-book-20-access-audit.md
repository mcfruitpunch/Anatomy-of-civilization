Related: #24. This note records a **new access route**, not an original-decree reading or proof of a payment.

https://louisianastatemuseum.org/louisiana-historical-center-colonial-documents

The museum explicitly lists **“1731 January–December | Black_Book_20_1731_Jan-Dec.pdf”** and links to:
https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/9bhpqkq0djd3zywa41lvq/Black_Book_20_1731_Jan-Dec.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=dmtth9s0tbkncfv9b62l9nrdx&st=y1xf6ufr

**Observed:** The museum's live finding-aid page and the linked Dropbox landing page were opened on 2026-11-06. The PDF's individual pages **were not read**. Its coverage label does not establish that a particular January 24 council act, February 24 royal decree, or debt clause appears in this volume.

**Institutional description:** The museum says the WPA Black Books are 1930s translations/abstracts of colonial documents, arranged chronologically; not every document has a corresponding WPA page, and some WPA pages refer to documents since lost. It also warns that older microfilm can be illegible and disrupt original order/provenance. This makes a WPA entry an **intermediary witness**, not an original French manuscript or evidence of a completed transaction.

Additional institutional guidance: Louisiana Colonial Documents Digitization Project, “Access”:
https://lacolonialdocs.org/post/9/access
The LHC describes the Black Books as a chronological index, with separate card/person and Louisiana Historical Quarterly finding aids. Its original French Superior Council records and Crown acts must not be conflated.

## Verification protocol (uncompleted)

1. Obtain readable PDF pages of Black Book 20 from the museum's published link or its reading room; record exact PDF image page, printed WPA page, heading, date, named parties, and any original-document identifier. Do not infer a match from a date alone.
2. Search for the **24 January 1731 local Superior Council record** (LHC 1731-01-24-03, https://lacolonialdocs.org/document/2003). Compare any WPA summary against the **24-page original French record**; record divergences and missing passages.
3. Separately seek the **24 February 1731 royal Company-debt decree**, Columbia Law catalogue record 1505745 (https://pegasus.law.columbia.edu/record/1505745), dated 1731 and printed 1733. A local judicial Black Book is **not presumed** to reproduce this royal decree.
4. Record distinct roles for lawmaker, tribunal, Company, claimant, party legally liable, and payee. An order, award, valuation, accounting entry or authorized medium of payment does not establish actual disbursement. Require a receipted instrument or comparable direct evidence before asserting payment.
5. Cross-check any claims affecting Choctaw and other sovereign Indigenous nations against community-authored histories, and distinguish colonial classification of enslaved people as property from their personhood, agency and lived experiences. Do not infer consent from colonial administrative records.

## Epistemic register

- **Documented (institutional finding aid):** The museum publishes a link labelled 1731 Black Book 20; its WPA series contains later abstracts/translations of colonial records.
- **Interpretation (methodological):** This offers a potentially useful parallel witness for local Superior Council material, not a replacement for original folios.
- **Unresolved:** Whether Black Book 20 includes the target January 24 act; whether any relevant abstract is complete or accurate; what the February 24 debt decree actually required; debtor/creditor identity, liabilities, settlements and payments.

No atlas event, monetary total, ledger arrow, named beneficiary, or new verified primary passage is warranted by this finding alone.
