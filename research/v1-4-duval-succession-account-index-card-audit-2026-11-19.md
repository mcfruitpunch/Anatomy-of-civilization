The LHC [digitization and indexing method](https://www.lacolonialdocs.org/post/8/the-process) says written preservation scans are 600 dpi and pages are scanned front and back. It also says initial indexing began in 2014 using WPA summaries from 1935–1942, with later corrections and added content. The Center warns that WPA abstracts contain numbering errors, inconsistent spellings and significant omissions of the names of enslaved people and women. This general description does **not** prove that this particular card was written by the WPA; its author and creation date are not identified on the image.

## Transcription of the English index card only

> #6558
>
> YEAR 1732 JULY 15
>
> STATEMENT OF ACCOUNTS DUE FROM THE IND. CO.
>
> Statement showing closing of account of Sr. Francois Duval, former Cashier-General of the India Company, with said Company as of 15 January 1736, showing a balance due the company by the Succession of Sr. Duval, of 101,975 livres 6 sols 2 deniers.
>
> (Signed) On July 15, 1732, by Bruslé.
>
> On January 15, 1736, by Prevost.
>
> Not printed in Louisiana Historical Quarterly.

Spelling and punctuation above follow the legible card text; this is not a transcription of the French handwritten account.

## Evidentiary assessment

### Documented about the record/card

- The LHC record page dates the French Superior Council item **15 July 1732** and displays five image assets.
- The first image is a typed English card #6558.
- The card's prose reports an account closure **as of 15 January 1736**, a balance of **101,975 livres 6 sols 2 deniers** due the Company by the succession of Sr. Duval, and sign-offs attributed to Bruslé (15 July 1732) and Prévost (15 January 1736).
- Four handwritten image derivatives are present and were opened; their text was not reliably transcribed.

### Internal contradictions / unresolved hypotheses

- The heading “DUE FROM THE IND. CO.” appears to point in the opposite direction from the prose, which says the balance was due the Company by the Duval succession.
- The catalogue date is 15 July 1732, while the card reports a closing/account date and second sign-off in January 1736. Possible explanations include a 1732 account later closed in 1736, a composite record, or an error in the card/catalogue. None is selected.
- The balance may represent an account claim/receivable if the card's prose is accurate, but the direction and legal status are not independently established from the underlying pages.

### Explicitly not established

- Original handwritten account entries or a verified composite balance.
- Legal debtor, creditor, estate representative, enforceability, collection or setoff.
- Any cash payment, receipt, settlement or transfer.
- Any Crown assumption of a Company liability.
- Any relationship to the separate **24 February 1731** Company-debt decree.
- Identity, genealogy or family relationships beyond the names printed on the index card.

No financial_flows.json record was created or changed. In louisiana_liability_ledger.json, doc-029 remains null for paid, transfer and liable; only its review label records that the English index card was transcribed while the manuscript remains unread.

## Affected-community context

This is an administrative account indexed through Company personnel. It is not a source for Indigenous consent or the experiences of enslaved people. The LHC's own methodology warning is important: WPA-derived abstracts may omit women and enslaved people's names. An index that names only Company officials and a succession cannot establish that such people are absent from the underlying manuscript or from the economic arrangements being accounted for. The card must not be used to infer that enslaved people were included in the account without reading the actual pages and identifying the transactions.
