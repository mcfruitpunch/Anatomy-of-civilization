# V1.4 evidence note: contemporary printed retrocession account (24 October 2026)

## Scope and source type

The March 1731 issue of *Mercure de France* carries a contemporary printed report of the 23 January 1731 Council decision concerning the Compagnie des Indes' Louisiana and Illinois retrocession. Source: https://bureaudumercure.org/mercure/term/keyword?keyword=13599 (keyword index/search extract, displaying printed pagination around p. 611). **Review level: indexed searchable transcription/excerpt; original printed page image and original royal decree articles NOT directly inspected in this milestone.** The transcription may contain OCR/typographic defects. Verify against a page scan before adopting exact quotations or detailed clause-level legal conclusions.

## Evidence separation

**Documented in the searchable contemporary account:** The decision is described as accepting the company's retrocession of the colonial concession and associated forts, buildings, artillery, armaments and troops, and ending exclusive company trade privileges. The account refers to free commerce for the king's subjects. This is the *colonial governing and trade privilege* dimension, not proof of a completed money transfer.

**Scholarly/contextual interpretation, not established by this extract:** Administrative retrocession and cessation of exclusivity may have affected creditor remedies and commercial competition. The mechanism, dates of actual enforcement, and distributional consequences require additional sources.

**Unresolved:** The February 24 debt decree's operative clauses, debtor and creditor roles, Crown assumption (if any), debt valuation, deadlines, individual settlements and receipts. Do not infer these from the January decision or from the word *payement* in the separate February decree's title. The 1731/1733 editions of the February decree remain uncollated: https://pegasus.law.columbia.edu/record/1505745 and https://heritage.bnf.fr/france-ameriques/compagnies-commerce .

## Affected communities and interpretive limits

The contemporary French royal account presents the Crown and the Company as the deciding parties. It does not establish consent by the Indigenous nations whose territories were claimed, nor describe the agency or experiences of enslaved Africans. Interpret alongside community-affiliated scholarship and Choctaw Nation historical writing already cited in the V1.4 notes, rather than treating the royal legal vocabulary of possession as morally or politically uncontested title. No Choctaw debt liability is inferred.
