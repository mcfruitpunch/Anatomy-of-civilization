# V1.4 evidence note: contemporary printed retrocession account (24 October 2026)

## Scope and source type

The March 1731 issue of *Mercure de France* carries a contemporary printed report of the 23 January 1731 Council decision concerning the Compagnie des Indes' Louisiana and Illinois retrocession. Source: https://bureaudumercure.org/mercure/element/text/2549547 (stable article record) and https://bureaudumercure.org/mercure/term/keyword?keyword=14671 (OCR passage). *Mercure de France*, March 1731, section **“Arrests, declarations, ordonnances, &c.”**, printed pp. **610–611**. The University of Fribourg's Bureau du Mercure project describes its searchable text as imported OCR layers, not a fully corrected diplomatic transcription: https://www.bureaudumercure.org/public/project/database.html . **Review level: indexed searchable transcription/excerpt; original printed page image and original royal decree articles NOT directly inspected in this milestone.** The transcription may contain OCR/typographic defects. Verify against a page scan before adopting exact quotations or detailed clause-level legal conclusions.

## Evidence separation

**Documented in the searchable contemporary account:** The decision is described as accepting the company's retrocession of the colonial concession and associated forts, buildings, artillery, armaments and troops, and ending exclusive company trade privileges. The account refers to free commerce for the king's subjects. This is the *colonial governing and trade privilege* dimension, not proof of a completed money transfer.

**Newly verified in the same searchable periodical text (25 October 2026):** Printed **p. 611** reports that the king *maintained the Company's rights against its debtors in the province* and permitted the Company to exercise those rights when it judged appropriate. A short OCR excerpt reads: “Maintient, S.M. ladite Compagnie dans les droits qu'elle a contre ses débiteurs de ladite Province”. This is a **reported retention of creditor/enforcement rights by the Company** against people owing *it* money, **not** a statement that the Crown assumed the Company's own debts, and **not** evidence that any provincial debtor actually paid. The OCR reading must still be checked against the page image and the underlying 23 January royal decree. See https://bureaudumercure.org/mercure/term/keyword?keyword=14671 , printed p. 611.

| Role / direction | What the 1731 periodical reports | Not established |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crown and Company | Retrocession of concession, governance claims and monopoly privileges; Crown as recipient of described royal assets | A cash price, inventory completion, or universal debt assumption |
| **Company as creditor** | **Retention of rights against its provincial debtors** (reported at p. 611) | Names of debtors, amounts due, collectability, court execution, or receipt of money |
| **Company as potential debtor** | **Not resolved by this January periodical excerpt** | Its obligations to suppliers, investors, workers or other creditors; whether Crown assumed any of them |
| Indigenous nations and enslaved people | Their voices and consent are not represented in this royal/legal summary | Consent, legitimacy of colonial title, or responsibility for corporate liabilities |

This asymmetry is essential: **“debts owed to the Company” are not “debts owed by the Company.”** No direction-of-payment arrows should be added to the atlas from a legal right alone.

**Scholarly/contextual interpretation, not established by this extract:** Administrative retrocession and cessation of exclusivity may have affected creditor remedies and commercial competition. The mechanism, dates of actual enforcement, and distributional consequences require additional sources.

**Unresolved:** The February 24 debt decree's operative clauses, *named* debtors and creditors, Crown assumption (if any), debt valuation, deadlines, individual settlements and receipts. Do not infer these from the January decision or from the word *payement* in the separate February decree's title. The 1731/1733 editions of the February decree remain uncollated: https://pegasus.law.columbia.edu/record/1505745 and https://heritage.bnf.fr/france-ameriques/compagnies-commerce .

## Affected communities and interpretive limits

The contemporary French royal account presents the Crown and the Company as the deciding parties. It does not establish consent by the Indigenous nations whose territories were claimed, nor describe the agency or experiences of enslaved Africans. Interpret alongside community-affiliated scholarship and Choctaw Nation historical writing already cited in the V1.4 notes, rather than treating the royal legal vocabulary of possession as morally or politically uncontested title. No Choctaw debt liability is inferred.
