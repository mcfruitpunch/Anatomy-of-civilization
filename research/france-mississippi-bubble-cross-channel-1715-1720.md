# V0.8 — France's Mississippi Bubble and Britain's South Sea System

**Historical period:** 1715–1720 · **Geographic scope:** France, Britain, French Louisiana and the Mississippi watershed · **Research status:** a source-graded opening inquiry, not a comprehensive archive review.

## The question

Did France's finance revolution under John Law inspire Britain's debt-conversion experiment? How did chartered trading monopolies and promotional narratives intersect with state borrowing and colonial expansion? What was actually observed, as opposed to a persuasive story imposed after the fact?

## Verified chronology and scope

**1715:** After the death of Louis XIV, the regent Philippe d'Orléans faced considerable public-debt and trade difficulties. The historian Arnaud Orain's BnF essay locates Law's rise in this context. [BnF](https://heritage.bnf.fr/france-ameriques/en/john-law-and-compagnie-doccident).

**1716:** John Law established **Banque Générale**, initially a private bank issuing notes. BnF Gallica links to the 1716 *lettres patentes* authorizing Law's bank. Do not call this the same legal body as the later Banque Royale without documenting its conversion. [BnF Gallica curatorial page](https://gallica.bnf.fr/selections/fr/html/droit-economie/grands-auteurs-de-la-pensee-economique-au-xviiie-siecle).

**1717–1718:** Law organized the Compagnie d'Occident and obtained the French Louisiana trade privilege. In 1718 New Orleans was founded after selection of a site with Indigenous advisers. French colonial narratives of abundant resources obscured preexisting Indigenous populations and the coercion accompanying settlement. [BnF Orain](https://heritage.bnf.fr/france-ameriques/en/john-law-and-compagnie-doccident); [BnF Vidal](https://heritage.bnf.fr/france-ameriques/en/new-orleans-1718-1769).

**1718–1719:** Law's bank shifted into the **Banque Royale** under royal control; archive summaries use somewhat different descriptions/timelines for the transition, so we don't assert a day. The company absorbed other French trading firms, becoming **Compagnie des Indes** by May 1719. The enlarged enterprise gained charter privileges in tobacco, indirect taxation and coin production. Some absorbed French colonial enterprises were involved in the slave trade. [BnF Orain](https://heritage.bnf.fr/france-ameriques/en/john-law-and-compagnie-doccident); [BnF catalogue](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb180021330).

**1719:** Government debt and company share subscriptions were linked. A Federal Reserve Bank of New York historical commentary recounts Law's aim to refinance **1.5 billion French livres** in public debt through a proposed issue of company shares. This is a claimed **program target**, not independently verified execution of the entire amount. It must **not** be compared numerically to British pounds without rigorous exchange, date and valuation work. [New York Fed](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2014/01/crisis-chronicles-the-mississippi-bubble-of-1720-and-the-european-debt-crisis/).

**1720:** Law became Controller General in January. Rising share prices, interventions in conversion between shares, notes and metal coin, and confidence in the money supply destabilized the system. Share and paper-note values declined sharply during 1720. Law left France in December. Economists dispute whether an otherwise rational fiscal reorganization was ruined by speculation and monetary decisions, or whether the system was structurally unworkable. [BnF Orain](https://heritage.bnf.fr/france-ameriques/en/john-law-and-compagnie-doccident); [François Velde, Cambridge chapter abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/origins-and-development-of-financial-markets-and-institutions/was-john-laws-system-a-bubble-the-mississippi-bubble-revisited/ABF3777E7BECD716068A1BD5D7069282).

## The cross-Channel question

Historians **William Quinn and John D. Turner** write that **British South Sea directors followed Law's debt-restructuring example** when advancing a comparable scheme for British public debt. [Quinn and Turner, 2020, *Boom and Bust*, chapter summary](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/boom-and-bust/1720-and-the-invention-of-the-bubble/3F45C87947B216514BE4F3AC9A1266D6).

**Trevor Jackson's** 2022 historical scholarship argues that the crises were connected across borders through finance, information and the movement of people. His chapter highlights investor James Brydges as a promising lead for independently verifying dated transactions. [Jackson, *Impunity and Capitalism*, chapter summary](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/impunity-and-capitalism/crisis-of-1720-and-the-invention-of-discredit/82AE19A790C8CEB8743DB19BD59FBF0F).

These published conclusions provide good reasons to pursue historical correspondence and share-ledger records. They do **not** by themselves document a meeting between John Law and John Blunt, a combined shareholder family, insider collusion, or orders issued across national boundaries. Evidence of parallel schemes, influence and conspiracy should remain separate.

## French Louisiana was not just a prospectus

Historian **Arnaud Orain** describes how the Company spread idealized maps and publicity making Louisiana seem extraordinarily profitable. He documents settlement ambitions, plantations worked by enslaved people, and the forced incorporation of Indigenous territory into imperial commercial claims.

Historian **Cécile Vidal** documents the selection of the New Orleans location with Indigenous advisers and the town's early dependence on enslaved Africans as well as Europeans serving indentures. These details matter because the capital narrative otherwise turns people into anonymous 'labor inputs.' [Vidal's BnF study](https://heritage.bnf.fr/france-ameriques/en/new-orleans-1718-1769).

The Mississippi Department of Archives and History reports that the Louisiana charter included an obligation to bring **3,000 enslaved people** during its term. This obligation must not be mistaken for a count of people actually transported or for proof of exact compliance. [Mississippi History Now](https://www.mshistorynow.mdah.ms.gov/issue/john-law-and-the-mississippi-bubble-1718-1720).

## Ethical and methodological rules

A company charter is not a secret plot. A debt-restructuring **target** is not a cash receipt. A promoter's attractive map does not verify the colonial resources advertised. 'People in France/Britain' does not designate a single homogeneous actor. Keep currencies in original units unless an actual historical conversion method is documented. Do not infer family guilt or ethnoreligious coordination from financial accounts.

**Research is cumulative and correctable:** original documents, translations, opposing interpretations and participant perspectives can change these records.
