# V1.4 — 4 August 1731 merchant-vessel exemption: a distinct legal instrument

Research date: 2026-11-04. Related: issue #24. **Evidence status: institutional catalogue examined; original three-page ordinance NOT read.**

## Verified bibliographic evidence

Columbia Law Library catalogues an ordinance **dated 4 August 1731**, printed in Paris by the **Imprimerie royale in 1731**, as a three-page online resource. Its catalogue title states that merchant ships outfitted and destined for Louisiana were exempted from carrying *engagés* and firearms for six years from the ordinance date. This is a **title-level characterization only**, not a verified transcription of operative clauses, implementation, enforcement, or actual voyages.

- Institutional catalogue: https://pegasus.law.columbia.edu/record/1505474
- Catalogue identifier: **LLMC1336596151**
- Wroth & Annan, *Acts of French Royal Administration*, **no. 1223**
- Catalogue states that LLMC Digital's PDF caption was viewed **19 July 2022**. That is the cataloguer's source-of-description date, **not our own reading**.
- The title gives **4 August 1731**; the catalogue gives publication year **1731**. These are distinct date fields.

## Relationship to the retrocession investigation

**Documented:** this is a separate 1731 royal ordinance concerning obligations imposed on merchant vessels bound for Louisiana. It is **not** the 24 February 1731 Company-debt decree, whose later 1733 printing is Columbia record 1505745 (LLMC1338688796): https://pegasus.law.columbia.edu/record/1505745.

**Interpretive research question, not a finding:** did the Crown's post-retrocession regulation change private merchants' obligations and colonial provisioning in practice? The title alone cannot establish why the exemption was granted, how many vessels used it, whether enslaved or free migrants were affected, or any change in corporate debts. Do not create a cash-flow edge or transfer-of-liability entry from this catalogue.

**Human consequences and archival silences:** `engagés` is a historical legal category, not an interchangeable term for enslaved Africans or Indigenous peoples. Examine the original ordinance and voyage manifests before identifying affected people or asserting coercion/consent. Seek community-grounded Indigenous histories and scholarship on enslaved and indentured people's experiences alongside royal policy records.
