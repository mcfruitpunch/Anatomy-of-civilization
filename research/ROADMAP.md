# Expanding the Civilization Atlas: research roadmap

This is a **research queue**, not a list of proven historical connections. New historical records require original scholarship and support from primary records. Source access and representativeness matter as much as the quantity of records.

## Research sequence (next 3 cycles)

1. **Complete the first two cases:** audit original archival passages and opposing historiography for the East India Company and 1953 Iran; add Indian, Iranian and other affected-population perspectives as sources permit. Separate colonial financial outcomes, political bargaining, local participation and retrospective interpretation.
2. **Extend institutions and governance across different world regions:** comparative research prompts include Song and Ming governance and merchant networks; the Ottoman fiscal and charitable systems; West African empires and trade; South Asian polities before colonial companies; Indigenous American political and land systems; premodern Islamic and European finance. Select specific cases only with credible source coverage.
3. **Model transitions:** trace changing political authority, taxation, commerce, military organization, resource control, and information systems. Compare cases instead of forcing them into a single master narrative.

## Planned platform features

- Historical date model for BCE, approximate ranges and uncertainty (current positive CE schema is insufficient).
- Time-aware relationship records with start/end dates and changing sovereignty/ownership.
- Geographic layers with cited historical boundaries, avoiding invented coordinates and static borders.
- Higher-quality entity-centric records with sourced aliases, succession and carefully restricted genealogy.
- Interactive method page explaining source types, claim statuses, corroboration and counterevidence.
- Contributor review workflow with anti-harassment safeguards, privacy protection for living relatives, and challenge/revision history.
- Broader accessibility checks including browser-level keyboard and mobile smoke tests.

## Ethical and analytic safeguards

- Document historical coordination where archival records actually show coordinated action; neither secrecy nor a list of attendees is sufficient alone.
- Do not draw family associations as evidence of shared objectives, criminality, or ethnic/religious agency.
- Show who benefited and who was harmed **without** automatically inferring who intended the outcome.
- Evaluate voices of affected people, language barriers, archival destruction and survival bias.
- Keep normative goals for regenerative futures separate from descriptive historical analysis.

## Acceptance standard

Every additional case needs: a clear question, chronological records with appropriate dating, relevant entities and specific relationships, verifiable sources and locators, alternative interpretations, and explicit gaps before appearing as 'documented'.
