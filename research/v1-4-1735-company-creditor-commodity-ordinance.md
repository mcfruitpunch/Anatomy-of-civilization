# V1.4 source verification — 30 March 1735 commodity-debt ordinance (27 October 2026)

## Why this is a new lead

The original **24 February 1731** Company debt decree's operative text remains unavailable for independent reading. This milestone instead identifies a later, specifically located colonial ordinance that can help test how *claims owed to the Company* were handled after the 1731 retrocession. This is **not** a replacement for reading the 1731 decree.

**Archival calendar, directly inspected:** Historic New Orleans Collection, *Guide to French Louisiana Manuscripts*, [notice 8949](https://surrey.hnoc.org/full_notice.php?id_document=8949). It dates a **three-page** Superior Council ordinance at New Orleans to **30 March 1735** and locates it at **ANOM A 23:118** (older notation AC A 23:118). The calendar describes an ordinance on payment of debts **to** the Company of the Indies, specifying acceptance prices for **furs, indigo, tobacco, cotton, pitch, tar and rice**. This is a **modern archival abstract** of an original ordinance, **not an original-French transcription**.

**Independent historical discussion, read in a searchable digitized edition:** Nancy Maria Miller Surrey, *The Commerce of Louisiana During the French Régime, 1699–1763* (Columbia University Press, **1916**), chapter XIII, **printed pp. 185–186**, reports rates for colonial produce at the royal storehouse on 30 March 1735 and cites **ANOM C13 vol. 20 fols. 208–212** and **A vol. 23, 30 March 1735**. [Public-domain 1916 scan bibliographic record](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_commerce_of_Louisiana_during_the_French_r%C3%A9gime,_1699-1763_(IA_commerceoflouisi00millrich).pdf). The book's text was examined in a searchable reproduction, **not independently checked against the original ANOM ordinance**. Do not assume every rate in Surrey is an operative clause of this exact ordinance until the manuscript is compared; do not mistake official rates for documented realized sales or payments.

This is a second, later indicator that the distinction between **debts owed to** and **debts owed by** the Company matters. The 1731 *Mercure de France* OCR account already reports retained Company creditor rights; it has **not** been matched article-by-article to the original decree. Do not treat the 1731 periodical and 1735 calendar as two receipts, nor draw payment arrows.

## Human consequences and source bias

The commodity list is not an account of who grew, gathered, transported or surrendered the produce. Colonial production was shaped by slavery, coerced labor, Indigenous trade and land dispossession. A French official's commodity valuation cannot establish Indigenous consent or represent the labor and autonomy of enslaved people. Surrey's early-twentieth-century historical language and official economic perspective must be contextualized with community-authored Indigenous histories, original testimony where available, and scholarship on enslaved people's experiences. In particular, do not assign Choctaw liability or collapse distinct Indigenous nations into an undifferentiated group.
